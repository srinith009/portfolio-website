How I got into automation

I started my B.Tech in AI and Machine Learning in 2024 expecting to spend my time on models and theory. What pulled me in instead was something more practical: noticing how much of a normal workday is people copying information from one place to another.

My first real automation came from my own frustration. I was spending more than four hours a day finding leads and writing emails one by one. So I connected Apollo, GPT-4 and Gmail into one pipeline, and that routine dropped to under twenty minutes. Getting a whole afternoon back is when I knew this was the work I wanted to do.

I care about automations that are dependable, not just clever: clear prompts, sensible error handling, and a human in the loop where it matters. Now I’m looking for a remote team whose slowest processes I can make run on their own.
